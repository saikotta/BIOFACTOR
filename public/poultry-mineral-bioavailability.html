Unlocking the minerals already in the feed.

Most of the phosphorus in grain and oilseed meal is locked in phytate, a compound birds cannot break down well. Phytate also binds calcium, zinc and iron. Farmers pay for minerals twice: once in the grain and again as supplements to make up for what the bird cannot use.

Mineral-solubilising bacteria in the gut produce phytase enzymes that break phytate apart, and organic acids that keep calcium and other minerals soluble where they can be absorbed. The bird gets more from the same feed, and less phosphorus ends up in the litter and the soil.

This has been shown in principle: when broilers on a phosphorus-deficient diet were given Lactobacillus strains engineered to produce phytase, they grew as well as birds on a diet with adequate phosphorus.10
